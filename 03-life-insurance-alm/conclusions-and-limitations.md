# Conclusions and analytical interpretation

## What the group recommended
The final client proposal was to:
1. Dedicate **€5bn** of current assets to a long-dated, cash-flow- and key-rate-matched fixed-income portfolio.
2. Allocate ten annual **€0.5bn contributions** to a diversified return-seeking portfolio.
3. Overlay approximately **€2.8bn 15Y + €0.3bn 30Y receive-fixed swaps** to reduce residual surplus duration risk.
4. Rebalance and implement a **years 11–15 de-risking glidepath** ahead of the major lump-sum election.
5. Monitor funding ratio, policyholder choices, market shocks and capital requirements; apply a 90/10 profit-sharing arrangement.

## What the results support
The presentation argues that a matched guarantee sleeve plus diversified surplus sleeve can combine contractual protection and long-horizon upside. Its simulated median 15-year IRR is described as approximately **6.0–6.5%**, while the risk analysis reports meaningful surplus-risk reduction from the interest-rate overlay.

These are **model outcomes**, not promised investment returns, demonstrated future funding certainty or a real-world guarantee of regulatory compliance.

## Where the model is most informative
- It explicitly maps **policyholder cash flows** into an asset-allocation and hedge decision.
- It distinguishes **asset volatility from liability-relative risk**.
- It tests how **election rates and long-tail mortality** affect cash-flow needs.
- It uses **key-rate DV01**, not only a single duration number.
- It connects return-seeking investments to a **surplus risk budget** and policyholder profit sharing.

## Where the model is not conclusive
- The final slides and underlying reports sometimes show **different IRR, VaR and funding figures** because they refer to distinct configurations or presentation summaries.
- Long-dated zero-coupon and ultra-long bond liquidity, spread and execution risks require independent implementation checks.
- A zero-PV-at-inception swap still creates collateral and liquidity needs.
- The Solvency II calculation is **illustrative** and does not constitute regulatory certification.
- A simplified cohort and payout election rule cannot capture the full range of insurance liabilities.

## Interview-ready explanation
**The investment problem was to meet a nominal guarantee without making the insurer's solvency depend on equity-market performance.** The group therefore separated liability matching from surplus generation, built a bond portfolio around future benefit cash flows and key-rate sensitivity, and used receive-fixed swaps to close remaining rate gaps. The central risk measure was **economic surplus**, not asset value alone. Monte Carlo, historical simulation and stress tests were used to evaluate whether the resulting strategy could tolerate adverse markets and different policyholder elections.

This is a collaborative case study. The explanation describes the group's analysis, not an assertion that one person developed every model component.
